## 3. The gap

**What every one of them misses:** a source for their numbers. Ghost states
"YouTube: 30%" for memberships with no citation; mediacube states subscriber
thresholds for Super Thanks/memberships without linking YouTube's own policy
page. None of the three is neutral, each is selling something adjacent
(cash advances, a publishing platform, MCN management), which shapes which
methods get the "top pick" framing.

**What we can answer that they structurally cannot:** we are not selling
any of memberships, Super Thanks, affiliate tools, or merch fulfillment, so
the piece can rank methods by real payout math (YouTube's actual stated
cuts, sourced from Google's own Help docs) without a vendor's thumb on the
scale. This mirrors exactly how the Demonetization/Policy cluster's edge
worked (primary-source verification over vendor-guessed numbers), not a
data-study edge, this cluster does not have a proprietary-data angle the
way the video-length/title-length studies did, and should not pretend to.

**Intent the SERP does not close:** none of the three gives a simple,
sourced "which of these actually pays enough to matter below 10K
subscribers" answer, mediacube's tier table is the closest but bundles its
own product pitch into the advice.
