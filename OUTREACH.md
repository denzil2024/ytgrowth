## How to vet a name before emailing (do this every time)

1. Open their most recent article. Confirm it's dated within the last ~6 months
   and they're still bylined at that outlet (people move jobs constantly).
2. Confirm the outlet has actually covered a data-driven third-party study
   before, not just opinion pieces. (This is what confirmed Hale — an actual
   past article covering someone else's data.)
3. Find their real email if listed, or use the outlet's general contact/pitch
   address. Do not guess an email pattern (first.last@domain) and send blind —
   high bounce rate, and it looks careless if wrong.

## Email templates

### Template A — for journalists/trade press (leads with the counterintuitive finding)

```
Subject: The "average YouTube video length" stat almost everyone gets wrong

Hi [Name],

I saw your piece on [their specific past article, e.g. the Little Dot Studios
whitepaper] and thought you'd want to see something we found in our own data.

We track YouTube upload data across ~30,000 videos published since Jan 2025.
The published "average video length" for a niche is almost always wrong,
because it's a mean, and livestream/lecture VODs sitting in a channel's
uploads feed inflate it badly. Gaming's average in our data is 73 minutes.
The typical (median) gaming video is 24 minutes. Education's average is
33 minutes against a median of 8.6 — a 3.8x gap.

Full breakdown by niche, methodology included: [link]

We also just measured actual upload cadence (not survey data) across 545
established channels — gaming channels post a median of 1.5x/week, well
under the "post daily" advice that gets repeated everywhere: [link]

Happy to share the raw per-niche numbers if useful for a piece, or answer
questions about methodology.

[Name]
[Title], YTGrowth
```
